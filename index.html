Our Story

San Francisco has always struggled with litter, but during the pandemic, the situation became untenable. While 311 handled most city issues, reports regarding sidewalk debris went unresolved. A deep dive into the municipal code revealed the hard truth: the Department of Public Works cleans the streets, but sidewalk maintenance is the legal responsibility of adjacent residents and businesses.

I initially tried to bridge the gap myself, even purchasing a commercial trash can for the corner to help contain the overflow. But the volunteer approach was unsustainable. The accumulation of litter was relentless, and asking neighbors to sacrifice their scarce free time wasn't the answer. We didn’t need charity; we needed a professional service.

I posted an ad looking for a partner and hired Manuel, a restaurant worker displaced by the shutdowns. The impact was immediate. Manuel didn't just pick up trash; he reclaimed the sidewalk, sweeping daily and managing bulk item reporting.

Neighbors noticed the clean pavement and immediately asked how to contribute. That was the spark for Clean Streets. I built a platform allowing residents to subscribe to professional neighborhood cleaning. This wasn't a donation to a faceless charity; it was a tangible investment in the community. We replaced sporadic volunteerism with a reliable engine: dedicated independent contractors funded directly by the neighbors they serve.

James Thompson Founder, Clean Streets

The Model

Clean Streets was born from a simple realization: while not everyone has the time to sweep a block at 6 AM, almost everyone has five dollars they’d gladly trade for a clean walk to the coffee shop.

We crowdsource small contributions from neighbors to hire dedicated staff at a living wage. It’s hyper-local mutual aid that scales. For over 5 years, this model has kept routes in the Mission consistently clean.

The Mission

This isn't just about litter. It's about dignity. When a street is clean, it feels safer. It feels cared for. It tells everyone who walks down it—residents and visitors alike—that the people here give a damn.

We aren't a massive agency with overhead and red tape. We are neighbors looking out for neighbors, restoring pride in our community one bag of trash at a time.
